The 72-hour checklist, layer by layer

PrepGuide builds this list from six quick answers about your household — how many people, what kind of home, your region, your budget — so the quantities below are a starting frame; your personal checklist carries the exact numbers.

  • Water: one gallon per person per day, for drinking and basic hygiene — three days, minimum, stored where you can reach it in the dark.
  • Food: shelf-stable meals nobody has to cook, plus a manual can opener. Food your family already eats, rotated so it never expires on the shelf.
  • Light: a lantern for the room and a flashlight per adult — batteries checked twice a year, when the clocks change.
  • Warmth: blankets per person and a safe way to warm one room if the heat goes out with the power.
  • Documents: IDs, insurance papers and one printed page of phone numbers, in a folder you can grab on the way out.
  • The go-bag: one bag by the door with the first night covered — change of clothes, medications, chargers, cash in small bills.

Warmth is the layer families under-plan. A house loses heat fast in a winter outage, and "we'll just bundle up" stops working on night two — which is why one small, dedicated heat source for a single closed room belongs on the list.
